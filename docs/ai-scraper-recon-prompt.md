# STEP 2 — HOW TO ANALYSE (the analyst method)

Don't conclude from one listing. **Sites are inconsistent**: one listing shows the energy certificate and the next doesn't; one shows the exact address and another only the district. Your conclusions must come from **comparing many listings**.

1. **Define objectives.** For each topic in Step 3, decide what you need to learn. Example: "Is the energy certificate given, in what form, and for which listings?"
2. **Build targeted searches** that isolate each objective, for example:
   - rent vs buy in a big city;
   - new-build only;
   - studios (0 bedrooms);
   - penthouses;
   - houses vs flats;
   - commercial / retail (if it exists);
   - rooms/share;
   - private advertisers vs agencies;
   - cheap vs luxury;
   - rural vs city centre;
   - recent vs old listings.

   Vary the cities and regions too.
3. **Open several random listings for each objective**, typically 5–10 and more when you see variation. Choose them from different positions and pages of the results, not only the first card. Over the whole analysis that usually means dozens of detail pages.
4. **Compare them.** For every model field, keep a **coverage matrix**:
   - field → present or absent in each sample;
   - where it comes from (JSON / DOM / meta / XHR);
   - the format variants seen.

   From that, conclude **ALWAYS / SOMETIMES / NEVER**, and **under which conditions**. Examples: "energy only on buy listings", "agency fees only on rent listings from agencies".
5. **Collect every distinct value** of anything enum-like: operation labels, property types, floor wordings, energy letters and formats, conditions, heating types, orientations, furnished states, feature labels, deposit wordings, availability wordings, advertiser types, precision levels. These become your **mapping tables** to our enums.
6. **For each field, evaluate every possible source** (JSON-LD, embedded state JSON, meta tags, DOM, background XHR, free text). Choose the most reliable one as primary, with fallbacks, and confirm across your samples that the chosen source gives the same value the page shows.
7. **Save evidence.** Save the HTML (`document.documentElement.outerHTML`) of representative pages you are already viewing into `src/test/resources/fixtures/`, plus any XHR responses you depend on (e.g. a phone, email or map JSON). They become the unit-test inputs. Keep at least one fixture per important variant.

Browse like a normal, attentive visitor, read-only:
- no login and no account creation, no contact forms, no saved searches;
- "show phone" / "show email" may be used **only to analyse their mechanism**, on a handful of listings (Step 3-F).

Get as much as possible out of each page (DOM, embedded JSON, network panel). If you meet a CAPTCHA or block, stop browsing, write down what happened (it matters for Step 3-H), and continue with what you have.

# STEP 3 — WHAT TO ANALYSE

## A. Search mechanics (Phases 1 and 2)

1. **Identity**: canonical base URL, country, currency, language (UI and descriptions), site alias `<site>-<cc>`, and whether the data is in the HTML or loaded by JavaScript/XHR.
2. **Search URL grammar**: how operation, location, every filter and the page number appear in the URL, and in what canonical order.
3. **Locations**: the hierarchy, and the **complete list** for the level used as basic splits (e.g. provinces), with URL slugs.
4. **Result count**: where the total number of results is shown, its format, whether it is capped ("1000+"), what a "no results" page looks like, and whether featured/promoted listings are included in the total.
5. **Filters**: the URL encoding and exact allowed values of each one, whether min-only and max-only ranges work, whether bounds are inclusive, what invalid values do, and whether listings without that attribute disappear when the filter is applied.
6. **Filter priority**: on the biggest location searches, which filter order gets every search below the per-search limit with the fewest steps.
7. **Pagination**: page URL format, maximum reachable page, order stability, and "nearby" results appended at the end. If a URL parameter sorts newest-first, check whether an edit or a renewal moves the listing back to page 1 (`LAST_MODIFIED`) or leaves it in publication order (`PUBLICATION_DATE`). That decides `newestFirstSort()` in Step 4.
8. **Composition of a result page (critical for Phase 0 and Phase 2).** On several pages of several searches, count every card and classify it:
   - organic results;
   - **featured / top / promoted extras** (often 1–3 per page, repeated on every page, or not part of the search);
   - ads;
   - project/development cards;
   - "nearby / similar" blocks.

   Determine the **real number of organic listings per full page** (`expectedListingsPerPage`) and exactly how to recognise and exclude the extras, so that `resolveListingIDs` returns precisely the organic listings. **Extras that inflate the per-page count make Phase 0 fail and pollute the splits.** Check the first page, a middle page and the last (partial) page.
9. **Result cards**: card and link selectors, a stable listing-ID regex, tracking params to strip, and the card price and image count. The card photo URL is also evidence for the thumbnail size (Step 3-D, Media): it is usually the same file as the detail photo with a smaller size token.

## B. Listing variants and site specificities

For **each topic**, use the method above and document:
- whether it exists on this site;
- how to **recognise it**, on the card AND on the detail page;
- what **differs** in the page and the data;
- how it **maps to our model**;
- the **code decision**.

1. **Operations: rent vs buy** (and room/flat-share, holiday/short-term, rent-to-own, business transfer, auctions).
   - Differences in URLs, filters and price meaning (monthly vs total, per week or night).
   - Detail-page sections: deposit, availability and rules for rent; community fees and mortgage for buy.
   - How the operation is identified reliably on the detail page itself.
2. **Residential vs commercial.** Offices, shops/retail, warehouses, garages/parking, storage rooms, land, whole buildings:
   - same section or separate?
   - in scope or excluded?
   - how detected, and how it maps to `TypeEnum` / `SubTypeEnum`.
3. **Property type and subtype (mandatory).** Produce a table: **site label/value → our `TypeEnum` + `SubTypeEnum`**, covering every value seen, including the ones you could not map. Find the most reliable source: a structured field first, then breadcrumb/URL, then title. Type and subtype must be resolvable for practically every listing.
4. **Floors**: ground, basement, mezzanine, "floor X of Y", **last floor / penthouse** (a floor category, `FloorNumberEnum.FLOORNR_PENTHOUSE`, not a subtype), and houses without a floor. Collect every wording. Set the numeric `floorNr` when the page states it. Set a category amenity yourself only when the page states one (`FLOORNR_PENTHOUSE`, `FLOORNR_GROUND`, `FLOORNR_BASEMENT`, and the lower/middle/high bands). Otherwise leave the category unset: `calculateAndSetFloorNrAmenity` fills it from `floorNr` and building height.
5. **Bedrooms, rooms and bathrooms.** **Studio = 0 bedrooms**: how is it shown? "Rooms" vs "bedrooms" (`roomsTot`)? Are toilets counted as bathrooms?
6. **Surfaces**: built vs usable vs plot area, and units.
7. **New build vs second-hand** and the condition values.
8. **Construction projects / developments** (one project grouping several new units).
   - How a project appears in results: one card, the units as separate cards, or **both, so units appear repeated**?
   - Does a project have its own page, its own ID space and its own price range ("from X")?
   - Are the units included in the result count?
   - Can a unit be linked to its project? Our model has `prjctId` / `prjctUrl` for that.
   - Decision: skip project cards, take only the units, or collect units from the project page, with a dedup rule.
9. **Duplicates and repeats**: the same property listed by several advertisers, promoted cards repeated on every page, "similar / nearby" blocks.
10. **Price variants**: price on request, "from" prices, price per m², fees included, negotiable, and **listings priced in a currency different from the site's**. A price drop ("was X") has no model field: report it as a gap.
11. **Status variants**: reserved, sold/rented but still visible, removed listings (status code, redirect, message), for `wasDelisted`.
12. **Lazy-loaded data**: anything that appears only after scrolling or clicking, or that comes from a background request. It feeds the AJAX captures.
13. **Anything else particular** to this site that the template does not anticipate. Document it and handle it.

## C. Priority fields: coordinates, address and precision (treat as critical)

Coordinates and address are the **most important** data after price, operation and type. Analyse them in depth:
- **Every possible source of coordinates**: JSON-LD `geo`, embedded state JSON, `data-lat/lng` attributes, the static-map image URL, inline scripts, and background XHR (often fired only when the map is scrolled into view).
  - Compare the sources across samples and pick the most reliable chain.
  - Check that the coordinates match the shown address (a rough map sanity check).
  - Watch for lat/lon swaps, and for the site's default city-centre point used when the address is hidden.
- **Address**: its structure and number of parts (street, number, neighbourhood/district, city, province, postcode) and how it varies between listings. Map it to `country / province / city / address / postcode`.
- **Precision level** (`Location.PrecisionLevelEnum`: `EXACT_PRECISE`, `STREET_PRECISE`, `POSTCODE_PRECISE`, `DISTRICT_PRECISE`, `CITY_PRECISE`, `PROVINCE_PRECISE`, `NOT_PRECISE`) and `hideStNr`: how the site signals exact vs approximate vs hidden locations. Record the **distribution of precision levels across your samples**. Use those enum constants. The serialized names are `exact`, `street`, `postcode`, `district`, `city`, `province`, `not_precise`.
- **`isPreciseLocation` (`location.setIsPrecise(...)`) — a site-relative decision.** When it is `false`, the later POI enrichment may skip the listing, so the threshold must fit this site:
  - If the site **often** gives street-number or street precision, then only EXACT/STREET count as precise, and POSTCODE or worse is `false`.
  - If the site **never or rarely** gives better than postcode, then POSTCODE precision is good enough to be `true`.
  - Define the rule from the measured distribution, document it in `RECON.md` with the numbers, and implement it in one place.
- **Decide** whether coordinates are always available. If so, they are mandatory: a missing value when the capture was requested throws `DetailParseRetryException`. The pipeline will not geocode the address later.

For **every field below**, determine with the method of Step 2: is it available, from which source, in which formats and variants, ALWAYS / SOMETIMES / NEVER, and under which conditions. Fields that are often *sometimes* present need extra samples. These are marked ⚠.

- **Basic**:
  - operation and **type + subtype** (mandatory);
  - `topTerm` (long/mid/short/lease) ⚠;
  - price;
  - currency and `originalPrice`: see "Currency" in Step 4. `originalPrice` is the amount in the site's currency, not a previous price.
- **Location**: see section C.
- **Property**:
  - bedrooms, bathrooms, `roomsTot` ⚠, surface (built/usable), floor number and floor category ⚠;
  - orientation ⚠, condition (`pStatus`) ⚠, view ⚠;
  - **energy certificate: letter (`energy`) and numeric score (`energyScore`) ⚠**, including "exempt / in process";
  - property levels ⚠, balcony size ⚠, and the amenities list (terrace, balcony, fireplace, storage room…).
- **Building**: year built ⚠, building height and units per floor ⚠, plot size ⚠, parking count and data ⚠, pool size ⚠, and facilities: lift, pool (private vs shared), garden (private vs shared), concierge, gym, security, accessibility.
- **Interior**: kitchen type ⚠, bathroom details ⚠, room sizes ⚠.
- **Furnish**: furnished / unfurnished / semi-furnished ⚠, appliances ⚠, heating system ⚠, A/C.
- **Transaction**:
  - **agency fees ⚠** (`saleAgencyFees` / `lTermFees`… via `setGeneralAgencyFeesFromValue` / `FromPercentage`);
  - **rent terms and term prices ⚠** (`lTerm` / `mTerm` / `sTerm`);
  - **security deposit ⚠** (months or amount);
  - **community/service charges ⚠**;
  - **availability date ⚠**;
  - **policies ⚠** (pets, smokers, students, families, bills included);
  - **requisites ⚠** (guarantor, payslips…);
  - utility costs ⚠, ownership/tenure ⚠, negotiable ⚠, and minimum/maximum stay (no model field: report it as a gap).
- **ContactExternal**: see section F. **Phone or email is mandatory.**
- **Two different IDs. Don't mix them up:**
  - **`contactExternal.extRefId` ⚠**: the **advertiser's own reference for the listing** (an agency's internal ID, e.g. "Ref: AB-1234"). It is usually different from the site's listing ID and is usually shown in a consistent place on the page (near the description or in the contact box, labelled "Ref" or "Reference"). Find where it is, how often it is present, and its format. Set it in `parseExternalReference` (as the template does). Never put the site's listing ID here.
  - **`scrapingDetails.externalId`**: the **site's own listing ID**, raw, without the site-alias prefix (the same value as `originalListingId` / `resolveIdFromListingUrl`). Always set it.
- **Display (commercial street-front data, mainly retail) ⚠**:
  - `facadeExp` (`FacadeExposureEnum`: street corner / street front / commercial centre / interiors);
  - `facadeSz` (facade length);
  - `pedestrEst` (`PedestriansEstimateEnum`);
  - `strtType` (`StreetPavementTypeEnum`);
  - `nearbyCom` (`NearbyCommerceEnum`).

  Rarely structured on sites. Look for them on shop/retail listings (features, "fachada", "esquina", "shopping centre", "pedestrian street", "high footfall") and fill them only when stated. If the site never offers them, say so.
- **Media**: gallery order, image count, a stable file key, floorplan ⚠, video ⚠, virtual tour ⚠, and listings without photos. **Both** `imagesArray` and `thumbImagesArray` are required whenever the listing has photos. Fill them in `MediaParser` with `addImageUrl` and `addThumbImageUrl`, same order, same `fileKey`, same caption. The first entry of each list is the main photo. Floor plans, logos, banners, map tiles and "similar listing" photos stay out of both lists.
  - **Full image** (`imagesArray`): the large photo the detail page shows as the main image. Keep the site's display size (often `original` or the fullscreen gallery size). Do not downscale it into the thumb slot.
  - **Thumbnail** (`thumbImagesArray`): a smaller rendition of **that same photo**, about **400×300** (a few hundred pixels on the long side is the useful range). A card sprite around 160×100 is too small to store. When the CDN offers several sizes of one file, pick the one closest to that range. Example: the same path with `?rule=web_160x108_ar`, `web_216x187_ar` and `web_482x269_ar` — store `web_482x269_ar`, not the two smaller ones, and not `rule=original`.

The path or id stays the same and a size token changes (`rule=`, `w800`, `/1024x768/`, `WEB_DETAIL`). Derive every thumb by swapping that token on the full-image URL. Use only a token you have seen the site serve. If the site publishes a single size, say so in `RECON.md` and leave `thumbImagesArray` empty rather than inventing a resize.
  - One thumb per full image. A listing with 17 photos has 17 thumbs, in the same order, with the same `fileKey` on each pair.
- **Description and dates**: the full description (original vs translated, "read more" content), and `created` / `updated` dates ⚠ and their formats (relative "3 days ago", localized month names).
  - **Project link**: `prjctId` / `prjctUrl` for a unit that belongs to a project ⚠. `scrapingDetails.parentListing` / `parentListingUrl` is a separate pointer for a child extracted from a parent page. Set the one that matches what you actually did, and say which in `RECON.md`.
- **Advertiser spoken languages** (`lngSpkn`) ⚠, if the site shows them.

## E. Share operation (rooms in shared flats), if the site has it

Analyse several room listings and determine what the site offers for:
- **`FlatMate`**: current residents (`residentAmount`), average age, gender of the flatmates or the accepted gender, occupation (students/professionals), extras (live-in landlord, couple, kids, pets), and lifestyle fields if published (smoking, cleanliness, schedules…). Open the enum and integer meanings in `FlatMate`.
- **`Transaction`**: accepted age range (`setAcceptedAge`), bills included (`utilCostVal`, the `UTIL_COSTS_INCLUDED` policy), deposit, availability, min/max stay (gap), and policies (smoking, pets, couples).

## F. Advertiser and contact details: agency vs private, phone and email

**A listing must have a way to contact the advertiser: a phone number (`extPhone`) or an email (`extEmail`), at least one of the two.** A listing for which neither can be obtained is **rejected** (not saved in the database, see the parsing rules), so it is reviewed and resolved later. This section therefore decides how many listings the scraper can actually store. Analyse it carefully.

### Advertiser type and details

- **Advertiser type** (`ContactExternal.usrType`: `PRIVATE` / `AGENCY` / `DEVELOPER`).
  - Every signal the site gives: a structured field in JSON, a "professional"/"private" label, an agency block with logo and profile link, an advertiser URL path (`/pro/`, `/agency/`…), developers/promoters and banks.
  - Compare several private and several agency listings.
  - Never default silently to PRIVATE when the block is simply missing.
- **Agency details**: name, logo (absolute URL), profile URL on the site, and the contact person's name.
- **Private details**: what is shown, e.g. a first name, a "member since" date, or nothing.

### Phone and email: where and how

For **both** the phone and the email, and separately for **private and agency** listings (they often differ), find out:
1. **Is it directly in the page?** In the HTML or the embedded JSON, a `tel:` / `mailto:` link, JSON-LD (`telephone`, `email`), a masked number ("612 34x xxx" that only reveals with a click), or an obfuscated email (encoded, split, or drawn as an image).
2. **Is it behind a reveal button** ("show phone", "show email", "call")? With the Network panel open, click it on a few listings and record:
   - the request it fires (URL pattern, method, parameters);
   - whether it needs cookies or tokens from the page;
   - the response format;
   - whether the same URL works as a **direct GET** with the listing ID (preferred) or only through the click.
3. **Is it on another page?** For example the agency's profile page on the site, which often shows the agency's phone or email even when the listing doesn't. Note whether it is per listing or per advertiser.
4. **Is it only reachable through a contact form or in-site messaging** (no phone or email ever shown)? Don't submit the form; just record that it is the only channel.
5. **Does it require a login?** If the site says "log in to see the phone/email", do **not** create an account or log in. Record:
   - what a logged-out visitor sees;
   - what the reveal request returns without a session (401/403, a redirect to login, an empty body);
   - what kind of account the site asks for (free user, paid, professional);
   - what the login page says it unlocks.

   Report it as a site-level finding: our engine has no logged-in session support today, so this is an **engine gap** for the developer to decide on.
6. **Quality of the value**: is it a call-tracking/virtual number (the same number for many listings of one agency, a site switchboard)? Is the email a real address or a site relay address (e.g. `xyz@reply.site.com`)? Record which kind you get. A site relay or switchboard is still stored, but noted.
7. **Did revealing trigger anything**: a CAPTCHA, a login wall, a rate limit, anti-bot cookies?

Then measure **coverage**: across your samples, the share of listings (private and agency separately) where phone, email or both can be obtained without login. These numbers go in `RECON.md`.

### Decide the implementation

- In the HTML/JSON → plain parsing.
- Known endpoint → an `AjaxCapture` with a `FETCH` trigger (preferred).
- Otherwise → an `AjaxCapture` with a `CLICK` trigger as a fallback.
- A `SCROLL_TO` trigger only when the request is IntersectionObserver-gated.

Chain `.usableWhen(regex)` on the `AjaxCapture` so an error or empty body is not accepted. The `key` is a stable substring of that request's URL (also the key of `result.getInterceptedResponse`). The engine is generic: the site declares everything in `getPhase3AjaxCaptures(String pageUrl)`. **AJAX captures only run in the browser modes (`BROWSER` / `DELEGATED`). HTTP mode ignores them.** Feed this into Step 3-H: if the contact is only reachable through a reveal request, that alone may require a browser mode.

Normalise what you store: the phone in a consistent international or national format (document which), the email lower-cased and trimmed.

### The contact rule, and the site-level verdict

- **Per listing**:
  - the capture was requested but failed (blocked or unusable response) → throw `DetailParseRetryException`, as for coordinates;
  - the listing genuinely offers neither phone nor email (or only behind a login) → log `[PARSING_PENDING]` with the reason and return `null`.
- **For the site as a whole**, state the verdict clearly in `RECON.md` and in your final message:
  - **OK**: phone and/or email obtainable for (almost) all listings;
  - **PARTIAL**: obtainable only for some listings, e.g. agencies but not private sellers. Give the share that will be rejected;
  - **BLOCKER**: not obtainable without login, or only through contact forms. The scraper would reject (almost) every listing.

  In the PARTIAL and BLOCKER cases, still implement the rule, but explain the options for the developer to decide: a logged-in session (engine gap), the agency profile page, a different source, or a decision to store listings without contact. Do not choose for the developer.

## G. Page UI (for the browser classes, if a browser mode is chosen)

The cookie-consent **reject** button; other pop-ups (save search, login, app banner) and how to close them; the text of the site's own block page; the host substring that recognises the site (`hostMatch`, usually the brand plus a dot). The same facts go in `BrowserBehavior` and in `SiteBrowserProfile`.

## H. HTTP or browser: assess `scraper.mode`

Decide with evidence, not by default. Our `HTTP` mode (`multi-scraper-framework/core/scraper-http/.../HttpClientService.java`) already behaves like a careful browser at the header level:
- realistic User-Agents from the Manager;
- headers consistent with that User-Agent (client hints, `Sec-Fetch-*`, `Referer`);
- a cookie store per proxy client.

Pacing is separate: `scraper.workers.request-delay-ms` (and the browser delay keys when a browser mode is chosen). The HTTP client does not execute JavaScript, solve JS challenges, or present a real Chrome TLS/HTTP2 fingerprint.

Assess:
1. **Is the data available without JavaScript?** Check view-source / raw HTML of search and detail pages, including the count, cards, coordinates, gallery and contact details (or a JSON endpoint reachable by plain GET).
2. **Anti-bot strength**: cookies, script domains and challenge pages. Protection that fingerprints TLS or JavaScript usually rejects a plain HTTP client quickly, even with realistic headers and cookies.
3. **If your environment allows it**, make a small series of plain HTTP requests from your shell with browser-like headers and a realistic pace: a search page, a detail page, a page N. Check whether you get full content, a challenge page, or a block. Record the results.
4. **Needed mechanics**: do required fields (coordinates, phone, email) depend on scrolling, clicking or in-page AJAX? Those need a browser mode.

Rule:
- **`HTTP`** when the data is in the raw HTML/JSON, plain requests are not challenged, and nothing essential needs page interaction.
- **`DELEGATED`** (the recommended browser mode) when anti-bot detection is likely or frequent with HTTP, when JavaScript rendering is needed, or when essential data needs scroll/click/in-page AJAX.

Record the reasoning and the evidence in `RECON.md`.

## Currency

The database always stores **USD**. `RealEstateParser.applyUsdPriceConversion` (inside `postProcess`) is the only conversion:
- the parser sets `basic.setCurrency(<site currency>)` and `basic.setPrice(<amount as shown on the site>)`, and leaves `originalPrice` null;
- for a non-USD currency, `postProcess` copies that amount into **`originalPrice`** and stores `price * <USD per 1 unit>` in **`price`**. The dialect loads that rate from Frankfurter (`UsdRateClient`). Do not read `scraperConfig.getExchangeRate()` for the stored price: the framework's rate is an EUR quote, and it stays `1.0` when the site currency is EUR;
- the template already overrides `getExchangeRate()`. Keep that override. It is the fallback when no `UsdRateClient` is wired (unit tests). An inconsistent pair (both fields set, and neither is the USD conversion of the other) makes `postProcess` return null, so the listing is dropped;
- keep `scraper.currency-rate-endpoint: http://127.0.0.1:${server.port}/api/v1/currencyRate`. The dialect answers `GET /api/v1/currencyRate/<code>` with `{"rate": <USD per 1 unit>}`. The framework calls it for every currency except EUR and aborts the run if the call fails. USD answers `1.0`.

After conversion, `resolveAndSetTopTermPrice` copies the **USD** `price` into the term slot of `topTerm` (`lTerm`, `mTerm`, `sTerm`, or `lease`). A null `topTerm` on a rent or share listing becomes `LONG_TERM`, and that slot is overwritten. Deposit amount, community charges (`commCharges`), agency-fee amounts, utility costs, and the other term slots are **not** converted: they stay in the site currency. Say so in `RECON.md` when you set them.

A listing priced in a currency **different from the site's currency** cannot be converted with the site rate: log `[PARSING_PENDING]` and reject it (see below).

## Parsing rules (mandatory)

- **Only set what the page states.** Null is better than a wrong value. The description resolvers run only after `parseListingDetails` returns a listing, and only on fields that are still null (see Step 1 for which fields they can fill). Never default a missing value to false or 0. Do not reject a listing because bedrooms, surface, or deposit are absent: those are optional, and a null lets the resolver try the description. Do reject when operation, type, or subtype is absent: no resolver fills those.
- **Use the best source per field** (as decided in Step 2), with fallbacks in order.
- **One feature item = one fact.** Never parse from concatenated text.
- **Mapping tables for enum-like values** (types, floors, energy, condition, heating, orientation, furnished, deposit, advertiser type…): a `switch` or map over every value you observed. **Every mapping must have a default branch that logs `[PARSING_PENDING]` with the raw value and the listing ID.** Values you observed only once, or are unsure about, are mapped but also logged as `[PARSING_PENDING]`.
- **Reject rather than store when something important is unresolved.** If `parseListingDetails` returns `null`, the engine counts the listing as failed and does **not** save it, so it comes back in the next run until the parser is fixed. Silently stored incomplete data tends to be forgotten. Therefore:
  - **Mandatory → log `[PARSING_PENDING]` + return `null`:**
    - operation, **type**, **subtype**;
    - the price, when one is shown but can't be parsed;
    - the currency (unknown or different from the site's);
    - **contact details: neither phone nor email obtained** (Step 3-F);
    - coordinates and address, when the site always provides them.

    When a requested capture failed (coordinates, phone, email), throw `DetailParseRetryException` instead, so the listing is retried.
  - The template already has this gate: `mandatoryGaps(...)` in `TemplatePhase3`, checked at the end of `parseListingDetails`. It covers operation, type, subtype and contact. Extend it with the site's own mandatory fields.
  - **Default for any other `[PARSING_PENDING]`** (an unmapped enum value, an unknown wording of a known field): also return `null`. Collect the pending issues during the parse in one small per-parse collector, log them together, and reject the listing at the end.
  - **Only explicit, documented exceptions may be stored with a pending log**: e.g. an unknown amenity label kept in `othrAmens`, or an optional field the site formats in free text. List each exception and why in `RECON.md`.
- **Log `[PARSING_SPECIFIC]`** (WARN/ERROR) when something this site ALWAYS has is missing (the layout probably changed): price, operation, coordinates if mandatory, the contact block, the features container, the gallery.
- Normalise text with `stripAccents` + lowercase, and parse numbers with `NumberParsingUtils`.
